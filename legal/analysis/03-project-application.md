## Approval sequence

1. **Purchase due diligence:** obtain deed/tax map, current survey, easements, title exceptions, official zoning confirmation, septic approval/as-built and design flow, well feasibility, utility capacity, flood/wetland/stream and watershed screening, and driveway/fire-access review.
2. **Town pre-application:** show the final Stage 8 plan, component uses, floor areas, sleeping/kitchen/plumbing program, staging, and conservative setbacks. Ask for written classification of the ADU, guest cabin, bathhouse, tubs, decks, and pavilion.
3. **Site validation:** survey the selected clearing and map contours, resource constraints, septic/well, access, utilities, snow storage, drainage, and fire apparatus needs. Reconcile the master geometry to real coordinates.
4. **Professional design:** retain the professionals required for architectural, structural, civil, septic, well, energy, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, and fire-safety work.
5. **Phase permits:** permit each stage against the final utility/site strategy so early trenching, service, septic, and grading do not foreclose later stages.

## Highest-risk assumptions

- The existing septic approval supports the final dwelling and sleeping-room load.
- A detached ADU can remain close to the principal home despite the posted multiple-principal-building rule.
- A plumbed, conditioned guest sleeping cabin is a customary accessory use rather than another principal dwelling.
- The selected clearing is outside or compatible with protected-stream, wetland, flood, steep-slope, high-elevation, and watershed constraints.
- The connected deck arrangement is acceptable for building separation, fire access, snow, drainage, and phase-by-phase inspections.
