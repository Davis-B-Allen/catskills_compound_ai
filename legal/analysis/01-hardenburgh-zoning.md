# Hardenburgh zoning analysis

**Analysis date:** August 23, 2026  
**Status:** Planning issue-spotting; not a zoning determination.

## What the posted law establishes

- Hardenburgh’s posted zoning law describes one town-wide residential district rather than mapped districts (Part 1, §2.3, PDF p. 5). **Text established.**
- The basic schedule states a 2-acre minimum lot area, 200-foot minimum lot width, 20-foot front, 30-foot side, and 50-foot rear building setbacks, and a 35-foot maximum height (Part 1, §3.2, PDF p. 7). Other provisions can increase these minimums. **Text established.**
- Natural-resource rules change development capacity and minimum lot treatment for wetlands/buffers, areas within 250 feet of a protected stream, large-pond proximity, flood hazards, elevation above 2,500 feet, slopes of 15-25 percent, and slopes above 25 percent (Part 1, §4.2, PDF pp. 9-11). Some categories restrict placement of principal structures. **Text established.**
- More than one principal residential building can be placed on one lot only if each residence could comply with area, bulk, and subdivision rules as if each had its own lot (Part 1, §5.1.1, PDF p. 14). **Text established.**
- Accessory buildings generally follow setbacks, with a stated exception allowing an accessory building in a required rear setback no closer than six feet to the property line (Part 1, §5.2.2, PDF p. 16). **Text established.**
- A building permit precedes work that requires one. A special-permit/site-plan use must receive written Planning Board approval and satisfy conditions before the Building Inspector can issue the building permit (Part 2, §§6.2.1-6.2.2, PDF p. 5). **Text established.**

## ADU uncertainty

Ulster County’s current ADU guide says Hardenburgh allows ADUs by conditional-use permit. The archived town zoning PDFs do not define “accessory dwelling unit” or plainly state that route. They instead define a dwelling unit as self-contained living space with complete cooking and sanitary facilities (Part 3, §10.2, PDF p. 9) and impose the hypothetical-subdivision test on multiple principal residential buildings.

**Project inference:** the proposed 440-square-foot detached ADU is a dwelling unit. Whether the Town treats it as an accessory/conditional use or as a second principal residential building subject to §5.1.1 is unresolved. Obtain the applicable local authority, submission standards, and a written pre-application interpretation.

## Guest-cabin uncertainty

The zoning law defines an accessory building/use as detached, incidental, and subordinate to the principal use on the same lot (Part 3, §10.2, PDF pp. 7-8). A dwelling unit requires both complete cooking and sanitary facilities. Omitting a kitchen may keep the proposed 192-square-foot guest cabin from meeting that dwelling-unit definition, but it does not automatically establish that overnight human occupancy is an allowed customary accessory use.

**Open:** ask whether a detached no-kitchen sleeping building with a bathroom, heat, electricity, and repeated guest occupancy is permitted as an accessory building, and what occupancy limitations or approvals attach.

## Setback-document conflict

The zoning law’s schedule says 20-foot front, 30-foot side, and 50-foot rear setbacks. Page 4 of the 2024 local permit application prints 25-foot front, 25-foot side, and 50-foot rear setbacks. The source set does not explain the difference.

**Working rule only:** reserve at least 25 feet front, 30 feet side, and 50 feet rear until the Town identifies the controlling values and measurement method in writing. This conservative envelope is not a legal conclusion.

## Implication for the example parcel

The parcel’s acreage exceeds the basic 2-acre minimum, but acreage alone does not establish buildable capacity. The prior proposal describes rugged terrain, leveled areas, and a seasonal stream. A current survey, boundary/right-of-way data, topography, elevation, wetlands and protected-stream review, flood mapping, and septic/well locations are needed before placing the compound. The close clustering of a principal home and detached ADU deserves early review under the hypothetical-subdivision rule.
