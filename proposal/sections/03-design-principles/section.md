## Design principles

### Geometry first, imagery second

The site model is the spatial contract. Generative images may improve material realism, vegetation, weather, and atmosphere, but they may not move structures, change roof types, invent deck edges, or reverse east/west relationships.

### Design the end state before Stage 1

Every structure, utility route, and major deck connection should have a reserved location before the first foundation is placed. Early construction should preserve the later plan even if buildout takes many years.

### One courtyard, one circulation system

The compound should read as a coherent cluster around a mostly flat outdoor room. Deck additions use broad, straight, obvious connections. Avoid fragmented pads, hairline joins, decorative zigzags, and unnecessary retaining work.

### Small structures with complete jobs

Each building has a clear program: primary dwelling, wellness, independent secondary dwelling, guest sleeping, or outdoor cooking. The collection should not depend on ambiguous “shed” labels to avoid permits or occupancy rules.

### Shared architectural language

- Near-black or charred vertical wood siding
- Dark matte standing-seam gable roofs
- Warm cedar-toned decking
- Dark window frames and restrained glazing
- Warm interior light against a subdued woodland palette
- Simple, durable landscape and drainage choices

### Evidence before commitment

Legal assumptions, cost ranges, septic capacity, and site conditions remain explicit until supported. The proposal should make uncertainty visible and convert it into a question, test, or professional task.
